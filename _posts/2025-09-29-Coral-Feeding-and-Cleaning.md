# Protocol procedure for feeding corals and cleaning coral plugs and side walls
  
## Feeding
Corals must be fed once a week. 
  
### Materials
- Corals in crates
- 4 Feeding Bins
- Coral Max (Coral Food)
- 1 tsp scoop
- 2 cup measuring cup 

### How To
1. Turn off the pumps to the system. Remove one half of the tank lids. 
2. Place 4 feeding bins on top of the tank lids that were not removed.
3. Pour 4 cups of water into each feeding bin. Make sure water does not get in between the cracks of the system. 
4. Take 1 tsp of coral food (Coral Max) and swirl into the feeding bin. 
5. Carefully place coral crates into feeding bins. Move corals into other feeding bins if there is not enough room for all of them to fit. 
6. Ensure each coral is submerged. Add more water if needed. 
7. Turn back on the pumps to the system.
8. Let corals sit for 10 minutes. 
9. After 10 minutes, turn the pumps back off. 
10. Remove the coral crates from the feeding bins and place them back into the system. Replace any corals that were moved back into the appropriate crate. 
11. Reswirl the food in the feeding bin.
12. Repeat steps 5 through 10. Corals can be fed with the same food for 2 rounds of feeding.
13. After 2 rounds of feeding, use the measuring cup and remove some water from the feeding bins. Pour down the floor drain. 
14. Pour the rest of the water out of the feeding bins down the floor drain.
15. Repeat this process for the rest of the corals in Blue Tank 1/2 and Blue Tank 3/4. 

## Cleaning Coral Plugs
### Materials
- Coral crates in system
- Coral crate replacement (should be the same number as the one you are cleaning in the tank)
- Toothbrush
- Bleach Bucket
- Tweezers

### How To
1. Remove the tank lid from the tank and place your tools in a reachable secure area.
2. Remove the coral plug from the crate, holding it by the base or sides of the plug. Try to not touch the coral itself. 
3. Remove algea on plugs by gently brushing over it with a toothbrush. Apply more pressure on the plug itself, but be gentle when removing algae from the corals. 
4. In cases where filamentous algea is found on coral plugs that cannot be removed with toothbrush, remove it by gently plucking it off with tweezers.
