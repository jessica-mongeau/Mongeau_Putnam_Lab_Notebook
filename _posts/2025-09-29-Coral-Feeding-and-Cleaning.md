# Protocol Procedure for Coral Feeding and Tank Cleaning
  
## Feeding
- Frequency: Once per week
- Duration: 10 minutes per feeding round
  
### Materials
- Coral crates (in system)
- 3 feeding Bins
- Coral Max (coral food)
- 5 cc scoop
- 2 cup measuring cup 

### Steps
1. Turn off the pumps to the system by flipping the labeled switches located behind the tank and remove one half of the tank lids. 
2. Place 3 feeding bins on top of the remaining tank lids and fill each bin with 4 cups of tank water. Avoid spilling water into the middle crack of the system. 
3. Add 1 scoop (5 cc) of Coral Max into each bin and swirl until mixed. 
4. Carefully transfer coral crates into feeding bins. If space is limited, move corals into additional bins as needed to ensure each coral is fully submerged. Add more water if necessary. 
5. Turn the pumps of the system back on and allow corals to feed for 10 minutes.
6. After 10 minutes, turn the pumps off again. Remove the coral crates from the bins and return them back into the system. Replace any corals that were moved back into the appropriate crate. 
7. Swirl the remaining food in the feeding bins to resuspend it.
8. Repeat steps 4 through 6 for a second feeding round using the same food.
9. After the second feeding, use the measuring cup to remove water from the feeding bins and pour it down the floor drain. Empty the remaining water out of the feeding bins down the floor drain as well.
10. Repeat this process for all corals in Blue Tank 1/2 and Blue Tank 3/4. 

### Cleaning And Storage
1. Rinse the feeding bins and measuring cup thoroughly with hot water at the sink. 
2. Place them on the drying table and allow them to dry for 24 hours. 
3. Put away Coral Max and 5CC scoop. If a new container of Coral Max was opened, label it with "Opened", the date and your initials. 
4. Replace all tank lids and turn the pumps back on. 

## Cleaning Coral Plugs
- Frequency: Once per week

### Materials
- Coral crates (in system)
- Replacement crates (same number as the one being cleaned)
- Toothbrush
- Bleach bucket
- Tweezers
- Putnam Lab Notebook
- Kim Wipes (if necessary)

### Steps
1. Turn off the pumps to the system by flipping the labeled switches located behind the tank and remove one half of the tank lids. Place your tools in a reachable secure area.
2. Retrieve a replacement crate in the clean crate bin (located near the curtain) for the crate in the tank you intend to clean. Ensure they are the same number crate. Place the replacement crate into the tank next to the one being cleaned. 
3. Carefully remove the coral plug from the crate, holding it by the base or sides of the plug. Try to not touch the coral itself. 
![Holding Coral Plug](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jessica-mongeau/Mongeau_Putnam_Lab_Notebook/master/images/HoldingCoralPlug.jpg
)
4. Remove algae on plugs by gently brushing over it with a toothbrush. Take the toothbrush and brush along the top of the plug where the coral and plug meet to remove any algae. Brush along the sides, base and bottom of the plug to ensure all algae has been removed.
![Cleaning Coral Plug](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jessica-mongeau/Mongeau_Putnam_Lab_Notebook/master/images/CleaningCoralPlug.jpg
)
7. In cases where filamentous algae is found on coral plugs that cannot be removed with toothbrush, remove it by gently plucking it off with tweezers. 
8. In cases where organisms, such as polycheate worms, are found on the coral plugs, remove them with tweezers and dispose with a Kim wipe. 
9. Place cleaned plug into replacement crate. Ensure none of the corals are touching each other or the side walls. 
10. Repeat steps 5 through 9 for the remainder of the plugs. 
11. Remove the empty, dirty crate from the system and submerge it completely into the bleach bucket located in the sink for 24 hours.
12. Record which crate was cleaned into the Putnam Lab Notebook and Coral Plug Cleaning Log (located on the refrigerator).
13. After every 3 crates, turn the pumps back on for at least 10 minutes before continuing to allow the water temperature in the tanks to regulate. 

### Cleaning and Storage
1. When cleaning is complete, submerge the toothbrush in the bleach bucket for 24 hours to disinfect.
2. Rinse the tweezers thoroughly with hot water in the sink. 
3. Return the lid to the tank and turn the pumps back of the system back on. 

## Side Wall Cleaning 
- Frequency: Once per week
- Note: This task is most efficient when performed during coral feeding, as fewer crates will be in the tank, making surfaces easier to access. 

### Materials
- Sponge
- Toothbrush

### Steps
1. Turn off the pumps to the system by flipping the labeled switches located behind the tank and remove one half of the tank lids. Place your tools in a reachable secure area.
2. Using a sponge, gently clean the sidewalls in a back and forth motion or in circular motions to remove accumulated algae. Avoid splashing water into the middle crack of the system.
3. Gently clean the filters with a sponge or toothbrush to remove any algae that has accumulated that may prevent water flow. 
4. Using a toothbrush, carefully clean the probe holder and probes to remove algae.

### Cleaning and Storage
1. Once cleaning is complete, submerge the toothbrush and sponge in the bleach bucket and allow them to soak for disinfection for 24 hours.
2. Return the lid to the tank and turn the pumps back of the system back on.
